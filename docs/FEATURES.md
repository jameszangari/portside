# Features

This page lists implemented capabilities. The Command Center panel with app, command, Settings and quicklink search is built (below); the Local Intelligence switch and Apple on-device model availability are built (below); Local Servers in Command Center (TCP listener search with copy, open, confirmed graceful stop and separately confirmed force stop after timeout) is built, with natural-language port questions when Local Intelligence is on (below); the standalone App Switcher with window selection, a window-first mode and title search is built (below); file search, clipboard and snippets, previews in the switcher, developer, menu bar, workspace, studio and local-AI features live in the [Portside 2.0 roadmap](ROADMAP_2_0.md) and are not part of the feature list.

Full feature list. The [README](../README.md) keeps a short summary.

## Reveal and hide

- Edge reveal driven by an `NSTrackingArea` on a thin edge window. No polling timers in the reveal/hide path.
- Non-activating panel: the first click works and the frontmost app keeps focus.
- Animated slide with a configurable duration, an instant option, and Reduce Motion support.
- Cancelable one-shot hide delay, canceled on re-entering the edge zone, the panel, or an open group grid.
- A group or folder opened with a click keeps the dock up after the cursor leaves, like the native Dock. Clicking anywhere else closes it, and the dock then hides with the normal delay. Grids opened by hover or a drag still close on their own.
- Dragging a file to the edge reveals the dock, and it stays up while the drag is over it. Hides with the normal delay after the drop or when the drag leaves.
- Timing logs for mouseEntered (or draggingEntered), animation start, and panel-interactive.
- Auto-hide toggle (menu bar item, separator right-click menu, Settings, or Cmd+Option+D). With hiding off, the dock stays revealed. New installs start with hiding off.
- With hiding off, windows stop at the dock like they do at the native Dock: a window edge that lands flush with the dock's screen edge (Rectangle Maximize or halves, Window > Fill, tiling) is pulled back to the dock's edge, about 65ms after Rectangle and 300ms after Fill's animation. Windows placed partly over the dock by hand are left alone. Turning hiding off fits windows already flush with the edge; turning it on, resizing the dock, moving it to another edge, or quitting Portside gives fitted windows their room back (or moves them to the new edge), like apps do when the native Dock shows, hides, or resizes. Settings opens centered in the space above the pinned dock. Toggle: Dock > Auto-hide > "Keep windows clear of the dock" (shown while hiding is off, on by default). It needs Accessibility: turning it on asks with the same grant helper as the tile menu (Open Accessibility Settings / Add This Copy), and while access is missing Settings shows Grant Access and Open Accessibility Settings. Granting or revoking access applies live, no relaunch.
- Sleep, wake, closing the lid and display changes: the panel and edge zones return to a known state, so the dock keeps working. With Hide the macOS Dock on, a crash or `kill -9` brings the native Dock back right away.
- Multi-display: an edge zone on every screen; the dock follows the display whose edge you hit. Works across Spaces and over fullscreen apps. Does not reveal while you are holding a mouse button (for example dragging a window); file drags to the edge still reveal.
- Notification badges, the same ones the macOS Dock shows, with no permission needed. They update while the dock is visible. Same size, placement, font and red as the native badge. Badge color is a setting (Appearance > Badges: macOS Red, Accent Color, system colors, or Custom from the Portside palette). Toggle in Settings. Group tiles show the sum of member badges.

## Apps

- Pinned apps with running indicators, updated from `NSWorkspace` notifications.
- Drag any tile to any slot: apps, groups, spacers, widgets, folders, and Trash can sit anywhere, in any order. Separators are items too: right-click one for Add Separator, Move Separator, Remove Separator, and Reset Layout. Trash can move but not be dragged out.
- Drag to reorder. Drag an .app from Finder onto the strip to pin it (a tile-sized insertion gap opens at the drop slot, same as folders).
- Drag an icon out of the dock to remove it, like the native Dock: the icon floats under the cursor for the whole drag (strip leaves a gap), a "Remove" label shows once it is clearly outside, and releasing it there removes it with a short poof (instant under Reduce Motion). Dragging back over the dock reorders as usual, and Escape puts it back. Works for app tiles, group tiles, and widgets (a group with apps in it asks first, see Groups); an app dragged out of an open group grid asks Remove from Group or Remove from Dock.
- Drop files on an app tile to open them in that app, like the native Dock. Only tiles whose app can open the files highlight and accept the drop (Finder takes folders). A not-running pinned app launches with the bounce.
- Running apps that are not pinned show after the pinned section.
- Activity icons on a running app's top-left corner: a green camera or orange microphone (the macOS privacy dot colors), an animated speaker while its audio output stream is active (Spotify and Music clear it as soon as they pause or stop; enable Measure actual playback to clear silent streams in other apps), or pulsing dots while it is Working (on by default for T3 Code, Claude, Codex; per app via Show Working Icon in the right-click menu). For T3 Code, Claude and Codex, Working follows the agent sessions the Agent Notch and Agents widget see, so idle CPU does not light it; they fall back to CPU only while neither is on. Other apps use busy CPU in the app or its child processes. One icon at a time in that order; group tiles show their strongest member. Each kind toggles in Dock > Activity Icons. Animations pause while the dock is hidden and stay still under Reduce Motion.
- Native Dock options (Dock > Apps): Show indicators for open applications, Show suggested and recent apps (up to 3 recently quit apps after the running apps), Make hidden apps translucent (Cmd-H fades the icon).
- Tile menu (right-click or hold), laid out like the native Dock: recent documents with file/folder icons, the window list (check on the current one), and the app's own actions (New Window, Spotify's Play) come from the native Dock's menu for that app; choosing one presses the real item. An Options submenu holds Keep in Dock or Remove from Dock, Open at Login, and Show in Finder. Portside additions stay top-level: Add to Group and Show Working Icon. Running apps also get Show All Windows (App Expose), Hide (Option for Hide Others) and Quit (Option for Force Quit). Needs Accessibility; without it the menu offers "Show Windows and Recents...", which can reset a stale post-update grant (`tccutil reset Accessibility`), quit Portside, and take a fresh System Settings enable on relaunch. An app that is neither running nor kept in the native Dock has no native section, so it shows Options, Portside items, and Hide / Quit when running. Force Quit on Portside's own tile skips the normal shutdown but still restores the macOS Dock. Open at Login for other apps uses System Events (may ask for Automation once); Portside's own tile uses the same SMAppService path as Settings.
- App tiles, group tiles, Portside's own tile and group cells: short click opens (or toggles the group); hold for 0.5s opens its menu (simultaneous with the tap, not exclusive); moving first starts a drag. Hover labels track the magnified icon's spring frames so they stay centered.
- Dock menus take the keyboard while open (Escape closes them, arrows move through them) without taking focus from the frontmost app.
- Separator right-click menu: Turn Hiding On/Off, Turn Magnification On/Off, Position on Screen (Left, Bottom, Right, checkmark on the current one), Add Spacer (Regular / Small), Dock Settings, Quit Portside.
- Spacers: empty gaps between pinned apps (like native Dock spacer tiles). Reorder by drag; drag out or Remove Spacer to delete. Imported from the macOS Dock on first launch and via Import.
- The app menu opens above the icon (beside it on the side docks), like the native Dock.
- Native Dock modifier clicks on app tiles and group grid cells: Cmd-click reveals the app in Finder (no launch), Option-click switches to the app and hides the one that was frontmost, Cmd+Option-click switches and hides all other apps. Portside itself is never hidden.
- Native-style launch bounce for pinned apps on any edge (sideways on the side docks) and for apps opened from a group grid (optional, off under Reduce Motion).
- Hover labels in a reusable non-activating overlay panel, styled as a speech bubble with a tail pointing at the icon, like the native Dock, using the dock's own background (style, frost and tint). A scrim under the text keeps it readable over any backdrop, with less of it the more the dock's own background already covers. Labels stay on one line up to 640pt, then wrap instead of truncating (only past four lines is the middle of a very long file name cut).
- "Add App to Dock..." from the menu bar item.

## Layout

- Bottom, Left, or Right position, switchable live.
- Drag the separator to resize icons live (clamped to what fits, persisted).
- Icon gap (0 to 8pt, default 6) between tiles, live in Settings; same gap on side docks. At 0, every item keeps the app-icon minimum spacing; separator air tracks the gap (5pt plus the gap per side).
- Magnification on every position: app, group, folder, Trash and widget tiles grow under the cursor with a smooth falloff to their neighbors, away from the screen edge (up on the bottom, sideways on the sides), past a shelf that keeps its thickness while it lengthens. Every tile grows by the same factor, so a Wide or Extra Wide widget stays proportionally wider. Near the ends of a long dock the shelf stays on screen. Separators and spacers keep their size and slide aside. Magnified size 16 to 128pt, set live in Settings. Hold Control-Shift over the dock to flip it for a moment. Labels, right-click, clicks and press-and-hold follow the magnified icon or widget, and a launch bounce plays at the magnified size. Off during drags and while a group grid or menu is up. Under Reduce Motion the size follows the cursor without the spring.

## Widgets

- Clock, Battery, Calendar, Weather, System Stats, plus optional CPU, Memory, Now Playing, Agents and AI Usage tiles.
- Now Playing (Spotify and Music): album art, song and artist. Extra Wide (default) shows art, song, artist and previous / play-pause / next; Wide shows art and the controls (song and artist in the hover label); Small shows the art. Clicking the tile toggles play/pause. Updates on each play, pause or skip from the players' notifications, and picks up a track already playing at launch. Asks once per player for Automation access; without it, song and artist still show (player icon instead of art) and the controls use the keyboard media keys. Hover shows song, artist, and Paused.
- Agents: Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode and T3-hosted sessions (including Cursor-in-T3). Small shows the provider's icon with a spinning ring and a count badge while agents work, and a green check once they are done. Wide adds the status ("2 working", "Done") and the app it runs in ("T3 Code +1"); the provider name, done count and project folders are in the hover. Hover lists each provider with its state and project. Click focuses the working agent's window (else the one that finished last): the exact tab in Terminal or iTerm (Automation, asked once), the window titled with the project folder elsewhere (Accessibility), or the host app itself (T3 Code). T3 Code runs and OpenCode sessions are discovered from their local SQLite databases (read-only, no tokens). Claude Code and Codex CLI sessions still need Agent Hooks once per Mac: Settings > Widgets > Agent Hooks (shown while Agents or Agent Notch is on). Other hooks are kept and each changed file is backed up first. The hook script and its folder are private to you (mode 0700, files 0600).
- AI Usage: 5-hour and weekly plan usage for Claude (from the Claude Code sign-in, via Anthropic's usage endpoint) and Codex (from its local session logs), and monthly quota for GitHub Copilot (premium requests, or Chat and Code on the free plan; the quota is account-wide, so it includes CLI and `copilot --acp` use). Copilot uses the GitHub CLI sign-in by default. A separate Allow Copilot CLI quota checks toggle enables its experimental headless source. Leave it off on managed Macs that block that executable. TLS trust failures direct users to IT; certificate validation stays enabled. Pick which providers to see in Settings > Widgets > AI Usage Providers or the widget's right-click menu; Claude and Codex are on by default, Copilot is off, and every provider picked shows on the tile. Small shows one ring per provider; Wide one gauge per provider (outer ring 5-hour or first quota, inner weekly or second quota, provider icon inside), plus labeled percentages when only one provider is picked; Extra Wide adds the percentages for two, and three gauges without text for three. Rings turn red from 90%. Hover shows only the percentages ("Session 79% · Weekly 71%", or "Claude 79% · 71%   Codex 12% · 30%" with several), no reset times. Refreshes every 5 minutes; click opens the first provider's usage page. Claude reads nothing until you agree: turning on the widget or the Claude provider shows a dialog saying Portside will read Claude Code's keychain token and send it to api.anthropic.com, and turning Claude off revokes it. Copilot works the same way: turning it on asks first, explaining the GitHub CLI token and optional Copilot CLI source, and turning it off revokes it. Portside does not run a Copilot CLI that macOS has quarantined, since that brings up the "Not Opened" alert; it uses the GitHub CLI, or says macOS blocked the CLI. After a failed run it leaves the CLI alone for 6 hours.
- A click on a widget still counts if the pointer moves up to 10pt during the press; past that it is a drag.
- Calendar connects from the signed app, explains failed requests, and opens Calendar privacy settings when access is denied. Permission changes are checked on click and refresh.
- Enable or disable in Settings or from the menu bar item; reorder by dragging in the dock.
- Every widget has a square (Small) design: an analog clock face, a Calendar-icon date with a dot for the next event, text-free concentric CPU (outer, blue) and memory (inner, purple) rings, and the existing Weather and Battery tiles. Side docks always use Small, so the column stays one tile wide.
- Hover labels show live readings: CPU / memory percentages, battery and charging state, weather temperature and location, clock date/time, and Calendar date/event or access status. Labels refresh with widget data. Click actions are unchanged.
- CPU, Memory and Battery show one large number without a tiny label or percent sign, sized to keep a clear gap from the ring (shared `RingGauge`). At 100 the number becomes a "full" symbol (full battery, filled CPU or memory chip); 99 and below show two digits. Charging shows as a green bolt badge on the ring's bottom-trailing edge, inside the tile's rounded corner. Battery ring and number color follow charge level: red near 0, amber at half, green when full (same scale whether or not charging). Below 28pt they switch to an SF Symbol in the ring (Battery picks a level or bolt glyph from charge state). Hover for the named percentage. System Stats keeps concentric rings; Wide adds color-matched numbers with neutral CPU / RAM labels. CPU and Memory start disabled and open Activity Monitor.
- Now Playing, Agents and AI Usage share one spacing rule (`WidgetLayout`): content sits the same inset from the tile edges at every size, rows with text use that inset for margins and gaps, and rows without text space their items evenly.
- Widget text uses system typography scaled from a 32pt tile reference, so the same size ratio holds across the icon-size range. At small dock sizes, Clock Wide shows just the time and Calendar shows the date with a status symbol and a red bar matching the date text width; secondary details stay in hover labels. Weather keeps its symbol and temperature.
- On the bottom dock, Clock, Calendar, System Stats and Agents can be Small or Wide; Now Playing and AI Usage can be Small, Wide or Extra Wide. Pick with the iOS-style size row in the widget's right-click menu (with Remove Widget and Edit Widgets...) or in Settings > Widgets.

## Groups

- User-defined groups of apps shown as one tile that opens a grid.
- Custom group icon: pick any SF Symbol (searchable by name and keyword, about 7,200 symbols) and a color from the shared palette editor (Symbol and Color tabs with a live preview) to replace the mini app grid on the tile. The picker shows under the name field when a group is created or renamed, from the tile menu (Change Icon...), and from Settings > Groups (click the group's icon, which shows a pencil on hover, or the Change Icon... button next to Ungroup and Delete). "Remove Icon" goes back to the app grid. Style per icon: System follows the macOS icon style (Default, Dark, Clear, Tinted) so it sits with the app icons; Light or Dark pins that one icon. A pencil beside the open grid's title jumps to the name and icon editor; a Done button (or Return) saves and goes back to the title.
- Inline naming on creation; rename from the open grid title (click it), the tile menu, or Settings. Ungroup and remove-from-group from context menus.
- Removing a group that has apps ("Remove from Dock", or dragging its tile out) asks first: Ungroup Instead (default, apps return as normal tiles), Remove Group, or Cancel. Empty groups go without asking. The alert gives focus back to the previous app.
- Optional open on hover, with configurable open delay and close grace.
- Dragging a file or app over a group tile opens its grid immediately. Drop an app from Finder, or drag a dock app (pinned or unpinned running) onto the tile or into the grid to add it (a dashed tile-sized gap follows the cursor so you choose the slot). Drop a file on a member that can open it (it highlights) to open it there. The grid closes when the drag leaves both the tile and the grid.
- Resting a file drag on a running app tile for about 600ms brings that app forward (native spring-load).
- Drag a member inside the open grid to reorder it. Dragging a member out asks Remove from Group (stays on the dock next to the group) or Remove from Dock, or Cancel.

## Appearance

- Icons match the macOS icon style (Default, Dark, Clear, Tinted) like the native Dock, and refresh when you change it in System Settings. Or pick "Original" to show each app's own artwork.
- One color picker for every color (background, tints, badges, group icons): preview and hex field, swatches in labeled Brand, Neutrals, macOS and RGB sections, Hue, Saturation, Tint, Shade, and Opacity where it applies, plus Reset Adjustments.
- Dock background, two styles that each keep their own settings, plus corner radius. Open group grids use the same look. Hover labels use an opaque native window background and primary text, keeping contrast over white or dark content regardless of dock transparency.
  - Liquid Glass (default). Match macOS is built to match the native Dock in dark mode: same color, rim, and no outer shadow, with the blur following the Liquid Glass slider in System Settings > Appearance. It has no settings; in light mode it uses the plain system glass. Custom starts from the same values and adjusts every part: follow macOS blur or set your own, refraction, saturation, brightness, contrast, a fill color with its own opacity, and the glass's own edge (Border) with its own opacity, width, and color. Color editors and color adjustments stay collapsed until opened, one at a time.
  - Solid: a color, linear gradient (with angle), or radial gradient; every color has its own opacity, plus overall Opacity, optional backdrop Blur (with a material thickness), and a border with its own width and color.
  - Reset applies to the current style only.
  - Text and dots on the dock follow the background, not just dark mode: a light solid color or tint gets black hover-label text, running dots, and grid labels; a dark one gets white.
  - Colors come from Portside's own palette instead of the macOS color panel: 20 preset swatches plus Saturation, Tint, Shade and Opacity sliders, inline in Settings. Gradients edit one stop at a time (click its chip). The custom badge color uses the same palette. Widgets keep their own frosted card whatever the background. Accessibility Reduce Transparency turns glass into a material live; Reduce Motion is respected for slides, springs, and the poof.

## Folder stacks

- Filesystem folders pin in the right section by default (after widgets and a separator, before Trash; drag to put it anywhere), separate from app groups. Drag a folder from Finder onto the dock to add one (an insertion gap opens at the drop slot, like the native Dock); reorder among stacks; drag out or Remove from Dock to unpin.
- Tile shows a mini grid of the folder's contents (the folder's own icon, with no card, when empty or with Display as Folder). Click opens the stack as a Grid, Fan, or List; click an item to open it, or drag it out to an app, Finder, the Desktop or a dock tile (the stack closes as it leaves). Fan matches the native one: no card, rows rise from the tile along an arc that bends right and tilts, names left of the icons, "Open in Finder" or "N More in Finder" on top, rows fly out in sequence; on a side dock it opens as a List. Menu: Open, Show in Finder, View as, Sort by, Folders First, Show Hidden Files, Display as Folder, Remove from Dock. The open stack's ... button pops the same AppKit menu (first click on Folders First and the other checkboxes fires). Settings are per stack.
- Sort by Custom Order (default: name, plus the slots you drop items into), Name, Date Added, Date Modified (newest first), or Kind. Drops choose a slot only in Custom Order with Grid or List; otherwise the item just lands in the folder.
- Drop files or apps onto a stack (or into its open grid) to move them into the folder (copy if move fails). Dragging over a stack tile opens its grid immediately. In the open grid, an insertion gap follows the cursor so you choose the slot; order is kept in dock.json. Contents update via FSEvents (no polling).
- Network shares: drag a mounted share (Finder sidebar or Desktop) onto the dock and it pins as a stack that remembers its server. Clicking it after the share disconnects mounts it again (saved Keychain password, or the system prompt) and opens the stack; if mounting fails, Finder gets the server URL.
- Network servers and links: drag a server from Finder's Network (or any `smb://`, `afp://`, `vnc://`, web URL) onto the dock to pin a link tile among the stacks, like the native Dock's url-tile. Click opens it (Finder connects to servers, the browser opens web links). Menu: Open, Remove from Dock. Reorder and drag-out work like stacks.
- Native Dock folder tiles from `persistent-others` import on first launch and via Import from macOS Dock, with their view (fan, grid, list), sort (name, date added, date modified, kind), and Display as Folder choice, and url-tiles import as link tiles. Plain file tiles are still skipped.

## Trash

- Trash stays at the far end on Bottom, Left, and Right, with native empty/full icons, magnification, and a hover label. "Show Trash in dock" (Dock > Layout) applies live and defaults on.
- Click opens Finder's Trash. Right-click or hold for Open and Empty Trash. Emptying asks for confirmation in Finder; empty or unreadable Trash disables the command.
- Drop files and folders from Finder to move them to the Trash. Dragging a dock tile onto Trash asks first: Remove from Dock, Move to Trash (apps and folder stacks), or Cancel. State updates use directory events, with no polling.
- Empty/full icon: prefers listing `~/.Trash` (Full Disk Access). If that is denied, uses Finder's trash count when Automation is already allowed. FSEvents watches the Trash folder (works without Full Disk Access). If neither read path works, the icon stays empty and a dismissible Settings hint links to Full Disk Access. Drops optimistically flip to full; Empty Trash flips to empty, then the watcher confirms.
- Empty Trash needs Automation permission for Finder. Denial and file-operation failures appear inline in Settings and in the Trash hover label. Under `swift run`, macOS may attribute permissions to the launching terminal; use the built app for permission testing.

## Settings window

- Sidebar panes support single-click selection and arrow-key navigation, with the selected row highlighted. Header and footer alignment follows the native row inset.
- System Settings-style layout: a sidebar with Dock, Appearance, Widgets, Groups, Command Center, App Switcher, Menu Bar, Agent Notch, and General panes, and the selected pane on the right, its title in the window toolbar with content scrolling under it. Changes apply live. Dock holds position, Trash, auto-hide, motion, app options (indicators, recents, hidden apps) and activity icons; Appearance holds sizes, icon style, background and badges; General holds startup, the shortcuts, hiding the macOS Dock, and import. Controls that only apply in some states (reveal and hide delays, magnified size, badge color) appear only then.
- Fixed 760 x 560 window (wide and short); long panes scroll instead of growing the window. Always opens centered on the primary display (same spot every time).
- While Settings is focused and auto-hide is on, the dock stays revealed for live preview; the toggle stays On. The Auto-hide pane notes this. Leaving Settings (or closing it) restores normal auto-hide.
- Sidebar shows the Portside icon and version (hover for author and build hash), with the app title aligned to the Dock pane's Layout heading, and a Check for Updates button pinned to the bottom corner with its result above it. It compares the latest GitHub release tag with the running version and says "Portside is up to date", or opens a modal when the release is newer. From an installed `.app`, Download and Install fetches the release DMG, verifies it (bundle id, version, Developer ID team), backs up dock.json, swaps the app in place with rollback on failure, and relaunches. The modal shows a progress bar with bytes downloaded, then the install and restart steps. A failure stays in the modal with Try Again and Open Release Page. Settings > General > Updates lists any other installed copies so a stale one can be removed; the section is hidden when there are none. Under `swift run` (or if the DMG asset is missing) the modal says why it can't install and offers Open Release Page. The browser never opens on its own. Checks and downloads are unauthenticated against the public repo.
- Settings > Widgets groups widgets (Time & Weather, System, Media, AI Agents) in a fixed order, so turning one off or on never moves its row. Each row is the same height: a live preview of the tile at its chosen size (a dimmed placeholder when off), the name, the size choice (shown, dimmed, even when off) and the switch. Reorder widgets by dragging them in the dock.
- Each tool pane lists its own modules; General lists the permissions. Each module shows its state (On, Off, Waiting with the reason, Failed with the reason), what it needs and what still works while it is off. Required modules show Always on; optional ones have a switch whose choice is saved, and turning one off stops it and cancels its work. Command Center, Local Intelligence, Local Servers and App Switcher are the optional modules. Each permission shows its live status (Allowed, Not allowed, Not requested, Unavailable, Unknown), what it is for and the fallback, with Request Access… only where a request is possible and Open Accessibility Settings for Accessibility. Nothing is requested when the pane opens; status is re-read when it appears and when Portside becomes active again. Unavailable permissions say "Unavailable on this Mac. Contact your administrator if it is managed." with no request button. Full Disk Access is listed as not required; Portside never asks for it, grants it or resets it.
- Set Up Permissions (Settings > General > Permissions): shows how many permissions are allowed and asks for the next missing one with one button, plus a button that opens its System Settings list. macOS lets only you turn these on, so Portside grants nothing; coming back from System Settings moves to the next one. Covers the permissions modules use (Accessibility, Screen Recording). Calendars, Automation and Full Disk Access keep asking from the features that use them.
- Local Intelligence (Settings > Command Center), an optional module, off by default and held off by restricted mode. Its section shows the provider and live availability (Off, Checking, Ready, Busy, Not supported, Unavailable, Not ready, each with the reason) and Check Again; availability is re-read when the module turns on, when the pane appears, and when Portside becomes active again, never on a timer. The provider is Apple's on-device model (Foundation Models `SystemLanguageModel`), and only that model: Private Cloud Compute and hosted models are never used and there is no fallback to another provider. Its status follows macOS: Not supported on a Mac that cannot run it or when the Mac's language is not supported, Unavailable while Apple Intelligence is off (including on a managed Mac), Not ready while macOS prepares the model (Portside never downloads it). Its only use is Command Center's port lookup (below): search and commands work the same with it off, and a port question then offers a numeric search. It is local only by design: no hosted AI requests, no cloud fallback, no model downloads, no runtime launches and no background inference. Turning it off cancels anything running and releases the provider.
- Restricted mode for work Macs (Settings > General > Work Mac) turns off and keeps off optional modules marked sensitive (scripts, capture, hooks, external runtimes, indexing, key interception); the dock, launching and Settings keep working. Turning it off turns nothing back on. It never changes macOS security, privacy or managed settings. Existing features (AI Usage, Agent Notch hooks, Weather) keep their own switches and are not affected.
- Global hotkey to open Settings (General > Keyboard), default ⌘⇧D, customizable, clearable. Menu bar Settings… still uses ⌘,.
- Command Center shortcut (General > Keyboard), default ⌥Space, customizable, clearable; registered only while the Command Center module is on (the row says so when it is off). A warning under it says when the chord is another Portside shortcut, an enabled macOS shortcut (Spotlight's ⌘Space and others, read only), or refused by macOS because another app holds it. Portside never changes those shortcuts. While recording, Portside pauses its own shortcuts so any chord can be captured, and a chord without ⌘ or ⌃ (other than ⌥Space) shows why it was not taken. A launcher that listens for keys without registering them (Codex, for example) cannot be detected; pick another chord if both open.
- App Switcher shortcut (General > Keyboard), default ⌥Tab, customizable, clearable; registered only while the App Switcher module is on (the row says so when it is off). ⌘Tab chords are refused with the reason (they belong to the macOS app switcher). The same read-only warning as Command Center's covers Portside's other shortcuts, enabled macOS shortcuts and a chord macOS refused.
- App Switcher shows (App Switcher pane): Apps (default) or Windows. "Search window titles with /" (default on) lets / start title search while the switcher is open. "Show window previews" (default off) shows a picture of each window in window rows; it needs Screen Recording, which is allowed from Settings > Integrations.

## App Switcher

- An optional module, off by default: turn it on in Settings > App Switcher. While it is off no shortcut is registered and nothing is tracked; turning it off releases the shortcut and closes an open switcher without switching. Restricted mode does not turn it off (a registered shortcut, not key interception, and it shows only app names, icons and window titles).
- A row of running apps, most recently used first, drawn to match the macOS ⌘Tab switcher: the same glass panel centered on the screen with no shadow, 128 pt icons on 120 pt squares 14 pt apart inside 28 pt of padding, the selected app on a plain gray square (lighter in Dark mode, darker in Light) and its name in 13 pt semibold under its icon, moved in from the panel ends when it is long. With more apps than fit, the icons and gaps shrink together and the panel stops 37 pt from each screen edge. Hidden apps look like the others (as in ⌘Tab) and are unhidden when chosen. Increase Contrast adds an edge to the selection. Native ⌘Tab is unchanged; this uses its own shortcut.
- Tap ⌥Tab to go straight back to the previous app; a quick tap switches without the panel appearing. Hold ⌥ and press Tab to move on, Shift-Tab or ` to go back (both wrap), or use the arrow keys, Home and End; let go of ⌥ to switch to the selected app. Moving the pointer onto an icon selects it, so letting go then switches to that app (an icon that appears under a resting pointer is not selected). Return or a click on an icon also switches. Esc cancels and leaves you in the app you were in, also while ⌥ is still held; so does clicking another app or another app coming forward. - Portside never takes focus while it is open: the app you were in stays active until you let go. An app that quits while it is open leaves the row; if the selected one quits, the selection moves to the app that took its place, and if every app quits the switcher closes. An app that quit before you let go is never activated.

Let go, Return or a click switches to that window. The window count is spoken by VoiceOver rather than shown, so the row stays like ⌘Tab. Without a window chosen it switches to the app, as before.
- Window-first mode (App Switcher shows: Windows): a list with one row per window, most recently focused first, so two windows of one app are two rows. Each row has the app icon, the window title (the app name for an untitled window), the app name, and Minimized, Full Screen, Hidden and the display's name (with more than one display), each as a word and a symbol. Apps with no listed window follow the windows ("No windows listed", or "Windows unavailable" for an app that is not answering). A quick tap goes to the previous window, which may be another window of the same app, and repeated quick taps alternate between the last two windows. Tab, Shift-Tab, the arrows, Home and End move, and moving the pointer onto a row selects it.
- Title search: while the switcher is open, press / . The panel shows at once with a search line, and the search stays open after you let go of ⌥. Type to filter (every word must appear in the window title or app name; case and accents ignored; results keep the recent order), Delete removes a character, ⌘Delete clears, Tab, Shift-Tab, ↑ and ↓ move, Return or a click switches to the selected result, Esc cancels and leaves you in the app you were in. Search lists windows in either mode. Turn it off in Settings and / does nothing.

Choosing a full screen window goes to its Space, as macOS does. Right before acting, Portside checks the window is still open and still belongs to the same launch of the app; a window that closed, or an app that quit or relaunched on the same process id, focuses nothing. Windows that close while the switcher is open leave the list.
- Window previews (opt-in, `switcherPreviews`): window rows (windows mode, the ↓ window list and search results) show a 64 × 36 pt picture of the window with the app icon in its corner. It needs the module on, the setting on and Screen Recording allowed; otherwise rows keep the app icon, and the panel says "Previews need Screen Recording. Showing icons." when the setting is on without the permission. Pictures are single stills taken only after the panel is shown and only for rows on screen (a quick tap captures nothing), at most 256 px on the long side, held in memory (12 at most) and dropped when the switcher closes, the setting or module is turned off, Screen Recording is revoked or the screen locks. The service is shared so Dock hover can reuse it; Dock hover does not show previews yet.
- Apps work without Accessibility, Screen Recording or Input Monitoring (only window previews use Screen Recording). Windows and titles need Accessibility; without it, both modes and search show apps (the panel says "Window titles need Accessibility. Showing apps."). Windows on other Spaces usually are not listed (the panel says so). Recent order starts when the module is turned on (until then apps appear frontmost first, then in launch order). Previews are a later roadmap item.
- VoiceOver: the panel is named App Switcher; each icon or row is a button with the app's name or the window title, app name and states (hidden, minimized, full screen, display, window count), a hint that ↓ chooses a window, the selected one marked selected, and the selection is announced when it changes while visible, including "Search window titles" and "No matches". ## Command Center

- An optional module, off by default: turn it on in Settings > Command Center. While it is off no shortcut is registered and the menu bar item is hidden; turning it off releases the shortcut and closes the panel. Restricted mode does not turn it off (it reads no data and intercepts no keys).
- A compact search panel for installed and running apps, Portside's registered commands (Open Settings, Toggle Dock Hiding, About Portside, Quit Portside), every Settings pane (Dock Settings, General Settings, ...) and your quicklinks. Open it with the shortcut (default ⌥Space; press again to close) or the menu bar item Command Center. It opens centered a fifth of the way down the screen under the pointer, over full-screen apps and on every Space, with no open or close animation.
- The app you were in stays active while you type; Portside does not activate. Esc or the shortcut closes it, and focus goes back to that app. Clicking another app or window closes it without taking focus back. A result that opens something (an app, Settings, a link, About) keeps that focus.
- Apps: apps in /Applications, /Applications/Utilities, /System/Applications (and Utilities), /System/Library/CoreServices/Applications and ~/Applications, one folder deep, plus any running app found elsewhere, each with its own icon. A running app says Running and Return switches to it (unhides it and brings it forward, opening a window if it has none, like a Dock click); otherwise Return launches it. The list is read in the background each time the panel opens (at most every 30 seconds), so opening never waits for the disk; a just-installed app appears a moment later. With an empty query apps are not listed until you have opened one from here.
- Settings rows open Settings on that pane. Quicklinks (Settings > General > Command Center) are a name and a link; only https, http, mailto and x-apple.systempreferences links open, and a refused or half-typed link stays listed, dimmed, with the reason (no scheme, another scheme such as file or javascript, no website address, a user name or password in the link). Links are checked again right before opening.
- Type to search, case and accent insensitive. Ranking is predictable: an exact title, then titles starting with the query, then every word starting a title word, then keyword matches, then initials ("vsc" for Visual Studio Code), then letters in order (three or more letters). Within a tier, results you open often or recently come first, then title order, then a stable ID, so the same query always gives the same order. The first result is selected on every change; a background refresh of the app list keeps your selection. The empty query lists recent and frequent picks first, then commands, Settings panes and quicklinks.
- Ranking runs off the main thread; each keystroke cancels the previous search and late results for an older query are dropped. Return, the arrows or Cmd+K pressed before the results arrive act on the latest query, never the previous list.
- History: only which result you opened, how many times and when (never what you typed or a link's address), kept on this Mac and cleared with Clear History in Settings > General > Command Center. It works without Accessibility, Screen Recording or any AI.
- Keys: Up/Down (or Ctrl+P/Ctrl+N) move the selection without wrapping; Return runs it; Cmd+Return shows its details; Cmd+K opens the Action Panel (the primary action, such as Run Command, Open Application or Open Link; Show Details; and Copy Command ID, Bundle Identifier, Path or URL) with its own filter field, and Cmd+K again closes it; Esc steps back one level at a time (Action Panel, details screen, query, then the window); Cmd+Esc closes from anywhere; Backspace or the back button leaves a details screen; Tab moves between the field and controls, not through results. While an input method is composing, Return and Esc go to the input method.
- An unavailable result stays listed, dimmed, with its reason under the title (for example "Portside is still starting." or a refused quicklink). Return shows the reason in the footer and runs nothing. A failure or cancel keeps the panel open with the reason; Esc while a command runs cancels it.
- Calculator and emoji (P2-CMD-06): type arithmetic such as `(1+2)*3/4` and the first row is the result; Return copies it as plain digits and closes the panel. Numbers, `+ - * / × ÷ ^`, signs and parentheses only (no functions, units or text evaluated as code); `.` or your region's decimal mark; dividing by zero shows the reason and copies nothing. `emoji rocket` or `:rocket` lists emoji whose Unicode name has a word starting with each word you typed; Return copies the character. Neither is stored or added to history.
- Snippets (P2-CMD-05): text you save in Settings > General > Command Center (a name and plain text, multiple lines and any language). Type its name in Command Center and Return copies the text exactly and closes the panel. It is only ever copied: nothing is run, expanded or pasted for you, and no Accessibility permission is needed. Stored on this Mac in `command-center-snippets.json` (owner-only), not in `dock.json`.
- Shortcuts (P2-CMD-07): an optional module (Settings > Command Center > Search sources, off by default, needs Command Center, held off by restricted mode because a shortcut can do whatever you built it to). Your Apple Shortcuts are listed when Command Center opens (at most every 30 seconds) and searchable by name; Return on one runs exactly that shortcut, with no input. Typing, searching and the on-device model never run one. One run at a time, and Portside waits at most 60 seconds; Esc stops waiting, though macOS may let a shortcut that already started finish. Uses the `shortcuts` tool that ships with macOS; without it the module shows why it cannot start.
- Files (P2-CMD-03): an optional module (Settings > Command Center > Search sources, off by default, needs Command Center, held off by restricted mode because it indexes). Add the folders to search in Settings > General > Command Center (Add Searchable Folder…); then type part of a file or folder name in Command Center. Names only, never contents. Return opens the item. Cmd+K lists Quick Look (⌘Y, a preview of the file inside the panel; ⌘Y or Esc goes back), Show in Finder (⌘⇧F), Open With and one row per app that can open it, and Copy Path. Portside never scans outside the folders you add, refuses the whole disk, system folders, volumes and your home folder, hidden files, and never follows symbolic links (it checks the path again right before opening). The index lives in memory, is rebuilt in the background only when a folder changed (macOS file events mark it; the rebuild happens when Command Center opens) or when you press Refresh, and is dropped immediately when you remove the folder or turn Files off. File names are not added to history.
- Menu Bar Items (P2-MENU-02): an optional module (Settings > Command Center > Search sources, off by default, needs Command Center and Accessibility, held off by restricted mode). The status items other apps expose through Accessibility are listed when Command Center opens (at most every 10 seconds, swept off the main thread with a 3 second budget) and searchable by title or app name; they never appear for an empty query. Return on one closes the panel and, 0.2 s later, opens it: one click at the item, with the pointer moving there and back for a moment, only when Accessibility confirms that item is what is at that point. An item hidden by a menu bar manager, clipped by the notch or covered is refused in the panel with the reason, and nothing is pressed (`AXPress` is not used: it succeeds without effect on some items). Portside re-reads the app first and refuses an item that changed or whose app quit; that failure beeps, since the panel is gone. Items with no open action (Control Center groups) are not listed and stay in the menu bar. It only searches and opens: it never moves, hides or reorders anything, shows no position or screenshot, and writes no other app's preferences. Without Accessibility the module waits and says so; it is not shown as an empty list.
- Menu Bar Layout (P2-MENU-05/06/07/09, experimental): Visible, Hidden and Always Hidden sections, per app on macOS 27. Automatic restores your saved order before each reveal; Manual leaves it alone. Grant `com.apple.MenuBar.plist` in Settings to restore without moving the pointer, otherwise Portside falls back to a Command-drag. Command-drag an item in the bar to show every section and move it. Named groups appear as a row in the Portside Bar and optionally as their own icon. Profiles save sections, order and groups, switch from the eye menu or a shortcut, and can apply automatically for a set of displays. Reset to Native restores the backed-up order.
- Portside Bar (P2-MENU-07, experimental): a panel under the menu bar that lists your hidden items, so they never need to fit in the real bar. Choose it in Settings > Menu Bar > Show hidden items, optionally only on notched displays, and pick where it opens: pointer or Portside icon, under the pointer, under the icon, left edge or right edge. Click an icon and that app's menu opens as a native menu under it, with the item still hidden, the pointer where it was and the bar still open. Apps that show their own window open it under the icon. Right-click for Open App and Move to section. Malwarebytes, PortKiller and BetterCapture only open from the real menu bar, so they show dimmed. Shows app icons, with no Screen Recording needed; menus are text only.
- Menu bar look (experimental): Settings > Menu Bar styles the Portside Bar (System, Match Dock or Custom) and the real menu bar (full bar, split at the notch or pills around items, all stopping at the notch and an attached Agent Notch, with the same Liquid Glass, frosted, color and gradient controls as the Dock, corner radius and shadow). The menu bar icon can be any SF Symbol from a searchable grid, your own uploaded image, or removed; without it, open your items by clicking any empty part of the menu bar, including the notch, or with a shortcut. The Portside logo menu can be hidden too. Always Hidden items only appear after a double-click on the menu bar or an Option-click on its icon. No extra permission. macOS still picks the color of menu bar text. Time Machine can be reordered within Visible, but macOS never hides it.
- Live Activities (experimental): Settings > Menu Bar can turn iPhone Live Activities on or off, right away. Standard hiding removes Live Activities while your items are hidden; Native hiding keeps them.
- Settings search: type in the field at the top of the Settings sidebar, or press Cmd-F, to find a setting by name or by related words. Picking a result opens its pane and highlights the section.
- Menu Bar Shelf on macOS 26 (experimental, hardware checks pending): the eye shows and hides items left of the divider. Icon customization and menu bar appearance are available, and profiles apply saved section assignments through verified moves. Click the eye or set a shortcut for basic reveal. Accessibility enables the layout editor and empty-bar gestures. Hold Command and drag an item across the divider to change its section. - Menu Bar hiding (experimental, hand gate open, macOS 27, install in `/Applications`): an open eye means your items are showing, a crossed eye means they are hidden. Also opens by clicking or scrolling the empty menu bar, an optional hover, and shortcuts. - Clipboard History (P2-CMD-04): an optional module (Settings > Command Center > Search sources, off by default, needs Command Center, held off by restricted mode because it watches the pasteboard). While on, text you copy is kept for 1 hour, 1 day (default), 7 or 30 days (up to 100 entries, 20,000 characters each) and found again by typing `clip`, or `clip` and some words, in Command Center. Return copies the entry back (nothing is pasted for you), Cmd+K deletes it. Stored encrypted (AES-GCM, key in your keychain), owner-only, the file excluded from backups, never synced or sent anywhere, never in logs. (A signed build keeps the key in the data protection keychain, for this Mac while unlocked; an unsigned build uses the login keychain, protected by your login password.) It skips text apps mark as secret or temporary, copies made while macOS reports secure input (a password field), file copies, images, password managers (built in) and apps you exclude, and what was on the clipboard when it started or while paused. Shortcuts and snippets are listed only once you type, never for an empty query.
- Local Servers: an optional module (Settings > Command Center > Search sources, off by default, needs Command Center, held off by restricted mode). Type a port (`5173`, `:5173`, `localhost:5173`) or the word port, server or localhost to list the TCP servers your account runs; after that, process names match too (`node`). Nothing is listed until you ask: one lookup per panel session, then when you pick Refresh or Stop Server (Return on the Local Servers row, or Refresh Servers in a server's Cmd+K actions). Closing the panel cancels a lookup and forgets the list, so the next session starts fresh. Each server row shows the port, process name, PID and where it listens (127.0.0.1, ::1, all interfaces; IPv4 and IPv6); one process on one port is one row, so two processes on a port or one process on two ports are separate rows. The Local Servers row says what happened: looking up, how many are visible to you and when they were listed, none visible to you (servers of other accounts and the system are never shown, so an empty list does not mean a port is free), some may be missing (cut short or unreadable entries), took too long, lsof failed, or turned off. Return on a server copies its address; Cmd+K adds Copy Port, Open http://… and Open https://… (you pick the scheme; Portside never checks or assumes that a port serves HTTP and never opens one on its own), Stop Server… and Refresh Servers; Cmd+Return shows its card (port, process, PID, address, each socket, start time, whether its identity was verified, owner, executable with your home folder as ~, its verified working directory, the project inferred from the nearest Git repository above it (or Unknown), and when it was listed). A server bound to all interfaces opens and copies on loopback (127.0.0.1, or [::1] for IPv6 only), never a network address; a server bound only to a network address is copied but not opened. A server that stopped since the list was taken is refused with "no longer listed". While server results are showing, Portside watches each listed process with the public macOS process-exit event and removes a card once the process is confirmed gone (or its pid now belongs to a different process); it also lists again in the background to find new servers, every 3 seconds at first, doubling to 30 seconds while nothing changes, and idles after 8 quiet listings until you press Return to refresh, a process exits or the query changes. Only one lsof runs at a time, a failed or timed-out background listing keeps the current list, and everything stops when the panel closes, the query stops asking for servers, or the module is turned off. Cards, port answers and the status row always show the same snapshot. Portside never connects to a listed process or reads its arguments or environment; it reads only the working directory, to name the project.
- Stop Server (P2-DEV-03): select one server, Cmd+K > Stop Server…. A native confirmation names its process, PID, selected port, owner UID, executable and other listed ports; stopping a process can stop all services inside it. Cancel is the default. Approval lasts at most 60 seconds and cannot be reused. Before SIGTERM, a new single-port listing and libproc reads must agree with the selected PID, start seconds/microseconds, owner and executable. Root, foreign owners, unknown facts, protected system paths, Portside, its parent and its direct helpers are refused. It sends SIGTERM once, waits up to five seconds for that process to exit, and reports stopped, still running, already exited, target changed, denied, protected or failed, with cancellation disclosed separately. Every completed action refreshes the session's server list; closing the panel or disabling the module invalidates pending approval. Cancellation after sending SIGTERM cannot undo it and is reported as an incomplete exit observation. No force stop, escalation, group signal or privilege prompt exists in this action. Project association and live exit events remain planned. The remaining macOS identity-read/syscall race is documented in [Architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md).
- Port lookup in Command Center (with Local Servers on). A bare port (`5173`, `:5173`, `port 5173`, `localhost:5173`) is looked up directly with no model. A question such as "What's using port 5173?" or "Show me port 3000", with Local Intelligence also on, is read by Apple's on-device model once you stop typing for a moment; Portside checks the port it proposes (in range, written in your question, the only number there) before it lists anything, then shows the same server card a numeric search shows, selected first, under a Port Lookup row that says the port was read on this Mac and the facts come from lsof. The model only names a port: the process, PID, owner and identity always come from the listing. The Port Lookup row says what happened: reading your request (Return searches the number now), looking up, how many servers are visible to you on that port, nothing visible to you on that port (other accounts' and system processes are not shown, so this does not mean the port is free), could not look up, which port? (no port, as in "What's running?", or more than one, as in "What's using ports 3000 and 5173?"), not a port lookup, or Local Servers off. When Local Intelligence is off, unavailable, not ready or gives nothing usable, the row says why in a line and Return searches the number you typed. Typing a new query cancels a question being read and drops its late answer; closing the panel cancels it too. Nothing is read or listed at launch, no question is sent anywhere but the on-device model, and a lookup never stops anything.
- VoiceOver: rows are buttons with title, module or unavailable reason, and selected state; selection moves, result counts, and reasons are announced. Follows the system appearance and Reduce Motion (the Action Panel appears without a transition). Rows take the first click while another app is active.

- Force Stop (P2-DEV-04): only the exact server whose graceful stop returned Still running offers Cmd+K > Force Stop (SIGKILL)…. A new native confirmation identifies the process name, PID, selected port, executable and other listed ports; it explains graceful termination failed, cleanup will not run and unsaved data may be lost. Cancel stays default. Approval expires after 60 seconds, all DEV-03 checks run again, and one explicitly approved positive PID receives SIGKILL once. A single-use Command Center intent is required; model output, a server key or PID cannot invoke force stopping through the registry. Any consumed attempt removes eligibility, including refusal or cancellation; another attempt requires another graceful timeout. Receipt and a fresh all-ports snapshot update server cards, numeric searches and natural-language port answers together, preserving unrelated listeners. No automatic escalation, group signal, child-tree termination, retry, saved approval or skip-confirm setting. Native keyboard/focus/VoiceOver behavior remains untested by physical interaction; build, isolated launch and automated safety tests are recorded in CHECKLIST.
