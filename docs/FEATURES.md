# Features

Full feature list. The [README](../README.md) keeps a short summary.

## Reveal and hide

- Edge reveal driven by an `NSTrackingArea` on a thin edge window. No polling timers in the reveal/hide path.
- Non-activating panel: the first click works and the frontmost app keeps focus.
- Animated slide with a configurable duration, an instant option, and Reduce Motion support.
- Cancelable one-shot hide delay, canceled on re-entering the edge zone, the panel, or an open group grid.
- A group or folder opened with a click keeps the dock up after the cursor leaves, like the native Dock. Clicking anywhere else closes it, and the dock then hides with the normal delay. Grids opened by hover or a drag still close on their own.
- Dragging a file to the edge reveals the dock, and it stays up while the drag is over it. Hides with the normal delay after the drop or when the drag leaves.
- Timing logs for mouseEntered (or draggingEntered), animation start, and panel-interactive.
- Auto-hide toggle (menu bar item, separator right-click menu, Settings, or Cmd+Option+D). With hiding off, the dock stays revealed.
- With hiding off, windows stop at the dock like they do at the native Dock: a window edge that lands flush with the dock's screen edge (Rectangle Maximize or halves, Window > Fill, tiling) is pulled back to the dock's edge, about 65ms after Rectangle and 300ms after Fill's animation. Windows placed partly over the dock by hand are left alone. Turning hiding off fits windows already flush with the edge; turning it on, resizing the dock, moving it to another edge, or quitting Portside gives fitted windows their room back (or moves them to the new edge), like apps do when the native Dock shows, hides, or resizes. Settings opens centered in the space above the pinned dock. Toggle: Dock > Auto-hide > "Keep windows clear of the dock" (shown while hiding is off, on by default). It needs Accessibility: turning it on asks once, and while access is missing Settings shows Grant Access and Open Accessibility Settings. Granting or revoking access applies live, no relaunch.
- Multi-display: an edge zone on every screen; the dock follows the display whose edge you hit. Works across Spaces and over fullscreen apps. Does not reveal while you are holding a mouse button (for example dragging a window); file drags to the edge still reveal.
- Notification badges, the same ones the macOS Dock shows, with no permission needed. They update while the dock is visible. Same size, placement, font and red as the native badge. Badge color is a setting (Appearance > Badges: macOS Red, Accent Color, system colors, or Custom from the Portside palette). Toggle in Settings. Group tiles show the sum of member badges.

## Apps

- Pinned apps with running indicators, updated from `NSWorkspace` notifications.
- Drag any tile to any slot: apps, groups, spacers, widgets, folders, and Trash can sit anywhere, in any order. Separators are items too: right-click one for Add Separator, Move Separator, Remove Separator, and Reset Layout. Trash can move but not be dragged out.
- Drag to reorder. Drag an .app from Finder onto the strip to pin it (a tile-sized insertion gap opens at the drop slot, same as folders).
- Drag an icon out of the dock to remove it, like the native Dock: the icon floats under the cursor for the whole drag (strip leaves a gap), a "Remove" label shows once it is clearly outside, and releasing it there removes it with a short poof (instant under Reduce Motion). Dragging back over the dock reorders as usual, and Escape puts it back. Works for app tiles, group tiles, and widgets (a group with apps in it asks first, see Groups); an app dragged out of an open group grid asks Remove from Group or Remove from Dock.
- Drop files on an app tile to open them in that app, like the native Dock. Only tiles whose app can open the files highlight and accept the drop (Finder takes folders). A not-running pinned app launches with the bounce.
- Running apps that are not pinned show after the pinned section.
- Activity icons on a running app's top-left corner: a green camera or orange microphone (the macOS privacy dot colors), an animated sound wave while it plays audio, or pulsing dots while it is Working (busy CPU in the app or its child processes, on by default for T3 Code, Claude, Codex; per app via Show Working Icon in the right-click menu). One icon at a time in that order; group tiles show their strongest member. Each kind toggles in Dock > Activity Icons. Animations pause while the dock is hidden and stay still under Reduce Motion.
- Native Dock options (Dock > Apps): Show indicators for open applications, Show suggested and recent apps (up to 3 recently quit apps after the running apps), Make hidden apps translucent (Cmd-H fades the icon).
- Right-click menu: Keep in Dock or Remove from Dock, Add to Group, Open at Login (checkbox), Show in Finder, then for running apps Hide (Show if hidden) and Quit. Hold Option and Quit becomes Force Quit, including on Portside's own tile, where it skips the normal shutdown but still restores the macOS Dock. Open at Login for other apps uses System Events (may ask for Automation once); Portside's own tile uses the same SMAppService path as Settings.
- App tiles, group tiles, Portside's own tile and group cells: short click opens (or toggles the group); hold for 0.5s opens its menu (simultaneous with the tap, not exclusive); moving first starts a drag. Hover labels track the magnified icon's spring frames so they stay centered.
- Dock menus take the keyboard while open (Escape closes them, arrows move through them) without taking focus from the frontmost app.
- Separator right-click menu: Turn Hiding On/Off, Turn Magnification On/Off, Position on Screen (Left, Bottom, Right, checkmark on the current one), Add Spacer (Regular / Small), Dock Settings, Quit Portside.
- Spacers: empty gaps between pinned apps (like native Dock spacer tiles). Reorder by drag; drag out or Remove Spacer to delete. Imported from the macOS Dock on first launch and via Import.
- The app menu opens above the icon (beside it on the side docks), like the native Dock.
- Native Dock modifier clicks on app tiles and group grid cells: Cmd-click reveals the app in Finder (no launch), Option-click switches to the app and hides the one that was frontmost, Cmd+Option-click switches and hides all other apps. Portside itself is never hidden.
- Native-style launch bounce for pinned apps on any edge (sideways on the side docks) and for apps opened from a group grid (optional, off under Reduce Motion).
- Hover labels in a reusable non-activating overlay panel, styled as a speech bubble with a tail pointing at the icon, like the native Dock, using the dock's own background (style, frost and tint). Labels stay on one line up to 640pt, then wrap instead of truncating (only past four lines is the middle of a very long file name cut).
- "Add App to Dock..." from the menu bar item.

## Layout

- Bottom, Left, or Right position, switchable live.
- Drag the separator to resize icons live (clamped to what fits, persisted).
- Icon gap (0 to 8pt, default 6) between tiles, live in Settings; same gap on side docks. At 0, every item keeps the app-icon minimum spacing; separator air tracks the gap (5pt plus the gap per side).
- Magnification on every position: app and group tiles grow under the cursor with a smooth falloff to their neighbors, away from the screen edge (up on the bottom, sideways on the sides), past a shelf that keeps its thickness while it lengthens. Near the ends of a long dock the shelf stays on screen. Widgets and separators keep their size. Magnified size 16 to 128pt, set live in Settings. Hold Control-Shift over the dock to flip it for a moment. Labels, right-click and press-and-hold follow the magnified icon, and a launch bounce plays at the magnified size. Off during drags and while a group grid or menu is up. Under Reduce Motion the size follows the cursor without the spring.

## Widgets

- Clock, Battery, Calendar, Weather, System Stats, plus optional CPU, Memory, Now Playing, Agents and AI Usage tiles.
- Now Playing (Spotify and Music): album art, song and artist. Extra Wide (default) shows art, song, artist and previous / play-pause / next; Wide shows art and the controls (song and artist in the hover label); Small shows the art. Clicking the tile toggles play/pause. Updates on each play, pause or skip from the players' notifications, and picks up a track already playing at launch. Asks once per player for Automation access; without it, song and artist still show (player icon instead of art) and the controls use the keyboard media keys. Hover shows song, artist, and Paused.
- Agents: Claude Code and Codex sessions. Small shows the provider's icon (the Claude or ChatGPT app icon) with a spinning ring and a count badge while agents work, and a green check once they are done. Wide adds the status ("2 working", "Done") and the app it runs in ("T3 Code +1"); the provider name, done count and project folders are in the hover. Hover lists each provider with its state and project ("Claude · 1 working · my-project", "+1" for more projects). Click focuses the working agent's window (else the one that finished last): the exact tab in Terminal or iTerm (Automation, asked once), the window titled with the project folder elsewhere (Accessibility), or the app itself (T3 Code has one window). The app is brought forward through Launch Services, like app tiles, since Portside is never the active app. Needs the agent hooks once per Mac: Settings > Widgets > Agent Hooks (shown while Agents is on) shows their status with Install Hooks / Remove Hooks. Other hooks are kept and each changed file is backed up first. The hook script and its folder are private to you (mode 0700, files 0600), and agent config files keep their original permissions.
- AI Usage: 5-hour and weekly plan usage for Claude (from the Claude Code sign-in, via Anthropic's usage endpoint) and Codex (from its local session logs), and monthly quota for GitHub Copilot (premium requests, or Chat and Code on the free plan; the quota is account-wide, so it includes CLI and `copilot --acp` use). Copilot comes from the Copilot CLI's own sign-in, or the GitHub CLI's when the Copilot CLI is not installed. Pick which providers to see in Settings > Widgets > AI Usage Providers or the widget's right-click menu; Claude and Codex are on by default, Copilot is off, and every provider picked shows on the tile. Small shows one ring per provider; Wide one gauge per provider (outer ring 5-hour or first quota, inner weekly or second quota, provider icon inside), plus labeled percentages when only one provider is picked; Extra Wide adds the percentages for two, and three gauges without text for three. Rings turn red from 90%. Hover shows only the percentages ("Session 79% · Weekly 71%", or "Claude 79% · 71%   Codex 12% · 30%" with several), no reset times. Refreshes every 5 minutes; click opens the first provider's usage page. Claude reads nothing until you agree: turning on the widget or the Claude provider shows a dialog saying Portside will read Claude Code's keychain token and send it to api.anthropic.com, and turning Claude off revokes it. Copilot works the same way: turning it on asks first, naming the CLI run and the GitHub CLI token, and turning it off revokes it.
- Calendar connects from the signed app, explains failed requests, and opens Calendar privacy settings when access is denied. Permission changes are checked on click and refresh.
- Enable or disable in Settings or from the menu bar item; reorder by dragging in the dock.
- Every widget has a square (Small) design: an analog clock face, a Calendar-icon date with a dot for the next event, text-free concentric CPU (outer, blue) and memory (inner, purple) rings, and the existing Weather and Battery tiles. Side docks always use Small, so the column stays one tile wide.
- Hover labels show live readings: CPU / memory percentages, battery and charging state, weather temperature and location, clock date/time, and Calendar date/event or access status. Labels refresh with widget data. Click actions are unchanged.
- CPU, Memory and Battery show one large number without a tiny label or percent sign, sized to keep a clear gap from the ring (shared `RingGauge`). At 100 the number becomes a "full" symbol (full battery, filled CPU or memory chip); 99 and below show two digits. Charging shows as a small bolt in the tile's corner, outside the ring. Battery ring and number color follow charge level: red near 0, amber at half, green when full (same scale whether or not charging). Below 28pt they switch to an SF Symbol in the ring (Battery picks a level or bolt glyph from charge state). Hover for the named percentage. System Stats keeps concentric rings; Wide adds color-matched numbers with neutral CPU / RAM labels. CPU and Memory start disabled and open Activity Monitor.
- Now Playing, Agents and AI Usage share one spacing rule (`WidgetLayout`): content sits the same inset from the tile edges at every size, rows with text use that inset for margins and gaps, and rows without text space their items evenly.
- Widget text uses system typography scaled from a 32pt tile reference, so the same size ratio holds across the icon-size range. At small dock sizes, Clock Wide shows just the time and Calendar shows the date with a status symbol and a red bar matching the date text width; secondary details stay in hover labels. Weather keeps its symbol and temperature.
- On the bottom dock, Clock, Calendar, System Stats and Agents can be Small or Wide; Now Playing and AI Usage can be Small, Wide or Extra Wide. Pick with the iOS-style size row in the widget's right-click menu (with Remove Widget and Edit Widgets...) or in Settings > Widgets.

## Groups

- User-defined groups of apps shown as one tile that opens a grid.
- Custom group icon: pick any SF Symbol (searchable by name and keyword, about 7,200 symbols) and a color from the shared palette editor (Symbol and Color tabs with a live preview) to replace the mini app grid on the tile. The picker shows under the name field when a group is created or renamed, from the tile menu (Change Icon...), and from Settings > Groups (click the group's icon, which shows a pencil on hover, or the Change Icon... button next to Ungroup and Delete). "Remove Icon" goes back to the app grid. Style per icon: System follows the macOS icon style (Default, Dark, Clear, Tinted) so it sits with the app icons; Light or Dark pins that one icon. A pencil beside the open grid's title jumps to the name and icon editor; a Done button (or Return) saves and goes back to the title.
- Inline naming on creation; rename from the open grid title (click it), the tile menu, or Settings. Ungroup and remove-from-group from context menus.
- Removing a group that has apps ("Remove from Dock", or dragging its tile out) asks first: Ungroup Instead (default, apps return as normal tiles), Remove Group, or Cancel. Empty groups go without asking. The alert gives focus back to the previous app.
- Optional open on hover, with configurable open delay and close grace.
- Dragging a file or app over a group tile opens its grid immediately. Drop an app from Finder, or drag a dock app (pinned or unpinned running) onto the tile or into the grid to add it (a dashed tile-sized gap follows the cursor so you choose the slot). Drop a file on a member that can open it (it highlights) to open it there. The grid closes when the drag leaves both the tile and the grid.
- Resting a file drag on a running app tile for about 600ms brings that app forward (native spring-load).
- Drag a member inside the open grid to reorder it. Dragging a member out asks Remove from Group (stays on the dock next to the group) or Remove from Dock, or Cancel.

## Appearance

- Icons match the macOS icon style (Default, Dark, Clear, Tinted) like the native Dock, and refresh when you change it in System Settings. Or pick "Original" to show each app's own artwork.
- One color picker for every color (background, tints, badges, group icons): preview and hex field, swatches in labeled Brand, Neutrals, macOS and RGB sections, Hue, Saturation, Tint, Shade, and Opacity where it applies, plus Reset Adjustments.
- Dock background, two styles that each keep their own settings, plus corner radius. Open group grids and hover labels use the same look.
  - Liquid Glass (default). Match macOS is built to match the native Dock in dark mode: same color, rim, and no outer shadow, with the blur following the Liquid Glass slider in System Settings > Appearance. It has no settings; in light mode it uses the plain system glass. Custom starts from the same values and adjusts every part: follow macOS blur or set your own, refraction, saturation, brightness, contrast, a fill color with its own opacity, and the glass's own edge (Border) with its own opacity, width, and color. Color editors and color adjustments stay collapsed until opened, one at a time.
  - Solid: a color, linear gradient (with angle), or radial gradient; every color has its own opacity, plus overall Opacity, optional backdrop Blur (with a material thickness), and a border with its own width and color.
  - Reset applies to the current style only.
  - Text and dots on the dock follow the background, not just dark mode: a light solid color or tint gets black hover-label text, running dots, and grid labels; a dark one gets white.
  - Colors come from Portside's own palette instead of the macOS color panel: 20 preset swatches plus Saturation, Tint, Shade and Opacity sliders, inline in Settings. Gradients edit one stop at a time (click its chip). The custom badge color uses the same palette. Widgets keep their own frosted card whatever the background. Accessibility Reduce Transparency turns glass into a material live; Reduce Motion is respected for slides, springs, and the poof.

## Folder stacks

- Filesystem folders pin in the right section by default (after widgets and a separator, before Trash; drag to put it anywhere), separate from app groups. Drag a folder from Finder onto the dock to add one (an insertion gap opens at the drop slot, like the native Dock); reorder among stacks; drag out or Remove from Dock to unpin.
- Tile shows a mini grid of the folder's contents (the folder's own icon, with no card, when empty or with Display as Folder). Click opens the stack as a Grid, Fan, or List; click an item to open it, or drag it out to an app, Finder, the Desktop or a dock tile (the stack closes as it leaves). Fan matches the native one: no card, rows rise from the tile along an arc that bends right and tilts, names left of the icons, "Open in Finder" or "N More in Finder" on top, rows fly out in sequence; on a side dock it opens as a List. Menu: Open, Show in Finder, View as, Sort by, Folders First, Show Hidden Files, Display as Folder, Remove from Dock. The open stack's ... button pops the same AppKit menu (first click on Folders First and the other checkboxes fires). Settings are per stack.
- Sort by Custom Order (default: name, plus the slots you drop items into), Name, Date Added, Date Modified (newest first), or Kind. Drops choose a slot only in Custom Order with Grid or List; otherwise the item just lands in the folder.
- Drop files or apps onto a stack (or into its open grid) to move them into the folder (copy if move fails). Dragging over a stack tile opens its grid immediately. In the open grid, an insertion gap follows the cursor so you choose the slot; order is kept in dock.json. Contents update via FSEvents (no polling).
- Native Dock folder tiles from `persistent-others` import on first launch and via Import from macOS Dock, with their view (fan, grid, list), sort (name, date added, date modified, kind), and Display as Folder choice. Plain file tiles are still skipped.

## Trash

- Trash stays at the far end on Bottom, Left, and Right, with native empty/full icons, magnification, and a hover label. "Show Trash in dock" (Dock > Layout) applies live and defaults on.
- Click opens Finder's Trash. Right-click or hold for Open and Empty Trash. Emptying asks for confirmation in Finder; empty or unreadable Trash disables the command.
- Drop files and folders from Finder to move them to the Trash. Dragging a dock tile onto Trash asks first: Remove from Dock, Move to Trash (apps and folder stacks), or Cancel. State updates use directory events, with no polling.
- Empty/full icon: prefers listing `~/.Trash` (Full Disk Access). If that is denied, uses Finder's trash count when Automation is already allowed. FSEvents watches the Trash folder (works without Full Disk Access). If neither read path works, the icon stays empty and a dismissible Settings hint links to Full Disk Access. Drops optimistically flip to full; Empty Trash flips to empty, then the watcher confirms.
- Empty Trash needs Automation permission for Finder. Denial and file-operation failures appear inline in Settings and in the Trash hover label. Under `swift run`, macOS may attribute permissions to the launching terminal; use the built app for permission testing.

## Settings window

- System Settings-style layout: a sidebar with Dock, Appearance, Widgets, Groups, and General panes, and the selected pane on the right, its title in the window toolbar with content scrolling under it. Changes apply live. Dock holds position, Trash, auto-hide, motion, app options (indicators, recents, hidden apps) and activity icons; Appearance holds sizes, icon style, background and badges; General holds startup, the shortcut, hiding the macOS Dock, and import. Controls that only apply in some states (reveal and hide delays, magnified size, badge color) appear only then.
- Fixed 760 x 560 window (wide and short); long panes scroll instead of growing the window. Always opens centered on the primary display (same spot every time).
- While Settings is focused and auto-hide is on, the dock stays revealed for live preview; the toggle stays On. The Auto-hide pane notes this. Leaving Settings (or closing it) restores normal auto-hide.
- Sidebar shows the Portside icon and version (hover for author and build hash) and a Check for Updates button pinned to the bottom corner with its result above it. It compares the latest GitHub release tag with the running version and says "Portside is up to date", or opens a modal when the release is newer. From an installed `.app`, Download and Install fetches the release DMG, verifies it (bundle id, version, Developer ID team), backs up dock.json, swaps the app in place with rollback on failure, and relaunches. The modal shows a progress bar with bytes downloaded, then the install and restart steps. A failure stays in the modal with Try Again and Open Release Page. Settings > General > Updates lists any other installed copies so a stale one can be removed; the section is hidden when there are none. Under `swift run` (or if the DMG asset is missing) the modal says why it can't install and offers Open Release Page. The browser never opens on its own. Checks and downloads are unauthenticated against the public repo.
- Settings > Widgets groups widgets (Time & Weather, System, Media, AI Agents) in a fixed order, so turning one off or on never moves its row. Each row is the same height: a live preview of the tile at its chosen size (a dimmed placeholder when off), the name, the size choice (shown, dimmed, even when off) and the switch. Reorder widgets by dragging them in the dock.
- Global hotkey to open Settings (General > Keyboard), default ⌘⇧D, customizable, clearable. Menu bar Settings… still uses ⌘,.

## Native Dock

- "Hide the macOS Dock" (Settings > General, on by default; the first launch shows the explanation and Cancel turns it off): keeps the native Dock from ever appearing while Portside runs, using the autohide-delay trick. Its exact prior settings are saved first and restored when you turn this off, quit, or stop Portside with Ctrl+C or `kill`. After a crash or `kill -9`, the next launch restores them first.
- First launch (no `dock.json`): pinned apps, folder stacks, position, and icon size are taken from the native Dock (Finder first). If a Hide-the-Dock backup exists, position and size come from it instead of the live values.
- "Import from macOS Dock..." (Settings > General > Import): a confirmation sheet shows what was found. Add missing apps (appended, nothing reordered) or replace the apps (groups and widgets stay; stacks merge or replace with apps), and optionally take the native position and icon size. Apps no longer on disk, spacers, and non-folder other tiles are skipped; folder stacks from `persistent-others` import. Your dock is copied to `dock.json.before-import` first, and "Undo last import" restores it. Read only: nothing in `com.apple.dock` is written.
- "Restore macOS Dock" in the menu bar item, and `portside --restore-dock` (restores from the backup and exits, no UI).

## Branding

- Liquid Glass app icon compiled from the Icon Composer document (`design/icon-composer/AppIcon.icon`). It follows the system icon style (light, dark, tinted) in Finder and the native Dock, and Portside's own dock tile, Settings header, alerts, and About panel use the same system-rendered icon, so it matches the size and look of every other app. Under `swift run` the icon is borrowed from a built `Portside.app` when one exists.
- Menu bar item uses the sail mark as a template image, so it follows light, dark, and tinted menu bars.
- "About Portside" in the menu bar item: the standard About panel with the version, author, and repo link.

## App bundle and startup

- `scripts/build-app.sh` builds a signed `build/Portside.app` with the brand icon, version from `VERSION`, and the Calendar / Finder / System Events Automation usage strings. `--install` copies it to `~/Applications`.
- App builds reuse a local code signing certificate after one-time setup, keeping the signing identity stable across rebuilds for macOS permission matching. Setup preserves the saved certificate fingerprint even when another certificate has the same name.
- "Open Portside at login" (Settings > General > Startup) through `SMAppService`. macOS is the source of truth; the toggle shows its real status, links to Login Items when approval is needed, and is disabled under `swift run`.
- "Open Settings when Portside launches" (Settings > General > Startup, `openSettingsOnLaunch`, off by default).
